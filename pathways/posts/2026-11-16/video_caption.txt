Every retelling edits the memory.

Researchers at Northwestern University had people learn where 180 objects belonged on a computer screen. The next day, people were tested on some of them. Nobody got every location exactly right.

On a final test a day later, people tended to place objects near the spot they'd chosen on the second day, not where they first learned them. The earlier mistake got carried forward. The lead researcher put it this way: "Your memory of an event can grow less precise even to the point of being totally false with each retrieval."

One honest limit: this was a lab task with objects on a screen, not memories of real life.

Here's the coaching part. Most of us have a few hurts we've told many times, to friends, or just in our own heads. It's fair to wonder how much of the telling is the moment, and how much is the last time we told it.

Try it: before you retell an old hurt this week, ask yourself, "Am I remembering what happened, or the last time I told it?" Then try starting with "The way I remember it..."

Source: Northwestern University (via ScienceDaily), "Your memory is like the telephone game" (Bridge & Paller, Journal of Neuroscience, 2012)
