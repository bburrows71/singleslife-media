Stuck in your head? Move first.

At an NIH Director's Lecture, NYU neuroscientist Dr. Wendy Suzuki described her research on exercise and the brain.

In one study, young adults rode an exercise bike for an hour or watched a TV show. The ones who biked improved their attention, and reported being able to focus better from about 30 minutes to 2 hours afterward.

In another, one group rode a bike three times a week for 12 weeks. Their moods improved significantly as their heart fitness improved.

One honest limit: Suzuki said we don't yet know the best amount of exercise. These are findings, not a prescription.

Here's the coaching part. When a decision or a hard conversation keeps looping, most of us try to think our way out while sitting still.

Try it: next time you're stuck, take a walk first. Afterward, ask yourself one question: what looks different now? Notice whether anything does.

Coaching, not medical advice.

Source: NIH Record, "Exercise Improves Mood, Focus, Aids Memory" (lecture by Wendy Suzuki, NYU, 2017)
