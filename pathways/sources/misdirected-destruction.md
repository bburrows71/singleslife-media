# Misdirected Destruction — Bill Burrows (Patterns), Pathways version

## Intro
- "Most people don't sabotage a good relationship because they secretly want to be alone."
- "When internal pain or numbness gets too loud, we panic." The relationship is the nearest thing to break.
- "Real healing doesn't come from finding a 'better' relationship next time."
## 1. The anatomy
- "Breaking a good thing feels strangely satisfying in the moment." Quick visible feedback feels like control.
- Faulty assumption: ending it stops the discomfort, but "the relationship was never what was causing the feeling in the first place."
- Afterward the pain remains, plus grief and regret.
## 2. Four ways it shows up ("The target changes. The mechanism underneath doesn't.")
- The sudden exit: withdrawing as intimacy grows.
- The fault finder: noticing flaws as things stabilize, building a case to leave.
- Misdirected destruction proper: starting a fight on your own terms.
- The wall: going silent until the other person ends it.
## 3. The shame loop
- Guilt feeds "broken/unlovable" beliefs, which make the next round likelier. "It's a loop, not a one-time event, and it keeps running until something interrupts it."
## 4. The takeaway
- "Destroy the habit. Destroy the old coping mechanism." "Just leave the good things standing."
- Past sabotage "doesn't make you unlovable."
- Bill: "I've lit that match myself, more than once."
