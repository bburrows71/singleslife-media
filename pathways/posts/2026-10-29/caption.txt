Your body reacts first.

When the brain senses danger, the amygdala sends a distress signal to the hypothalamus, which works like a command center and switches on the body's stress response. Harvard Health notes this wiring is so fast it can start before the brain's visual centers have fully processed what's happening. That's how people jump out of the way of a car before they think about it.

Useful in traffic. Less useful in a tense conversation, where your body may be braced before you've decided anything.

Try it: next time a moment heats up, notice the first signal in your body. A tight jaw, a hot face, talking faster. Name it. Take a few slow breaths from the belly (Harvard Health lists deep abdominal breathing as one way to help the body calm down). Then choose what to say.

Coaching, not medical advice.

Source: Harvard Health Publishing, "Understanding the stress response"
