Give a new habit a moment to live in.

Researchers at University College London (Lally and colleagues, European Journal of Social Psychology) followed people as they tried to build a new habit. They describe a habit as an action that becomes automatic after frequent repetition: a cue in a situation gets linked to the behavior.

What helped: repeating the behavior consistently in the same context. Missing a single chance didn't significantly set people back. People who were very inconsistent didn't form the habit.

And it took longer than most people expect. On average, 66 days for the people whose data fit the researchers' model, and some took longer.

Here's the coaching part. Most of us pick the habit and skip the moment. "Be more patient" has no address. "When I feel my jaw tighten, I take one breath before I answer" does.

Try it: pick one inner habit you want. Finish the sentence: "When ___ happens, I will ___." Then give it a couple of months before you judge it.

Source: UCL News, "How long does it take to form a habit?" (Lally et al., 2009)
