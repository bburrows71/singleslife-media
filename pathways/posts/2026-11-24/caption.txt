Every complaint hides a request.

"You never call." "You're always on your phone." "You didn't even ask how my day was."

When someone's upset with you, it usually lands like an attack. The first urge is to defend: the dates, the reasons, the times you did call.

But under most complaints is a request that didn't come out plainly. "You never call" can mean "I miss you." "You're always on your phone" can mean "I want your attention."

You can't always know which. So ask instead of guessing: "What would you like from me here?" Then listen to the answer, even if it's not what you expected.

Try it this week: next time someone's upset with you, write down their complaint. Next to it, write the request you hear inside it. Then check it with them.

Noticing the request doesn't mean you have to say yes. It means you're answering the real question.
