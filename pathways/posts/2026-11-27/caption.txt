Is willpower really limited?

A lot of us carry an idea about willpower: you get a fixed amount each day, and once it's used up, it's gone.

Stanford researchers (Job, Dweck and Walton, Psychological Science, 2010) ran four experiments to test and change students' beliefs about willpower. After a tiring task, the students who believed willpower is a limited resource, or were led to believe it, did worse on standard concentration tests. In the researchers' summary, a person's "mindset and personal beliefs about willpower" shaped how long and how well they could work on a tough mental task.

One honest limit: these were experiments with college students. It's a finding about beliefs, not proof that rest doesn't matter. Sometimes you really are tired.

Here's the coaching part. "I'm out of willpower" can be a fact. It can also be a story that quietly gives us permission to stop right where things get hard.

Try it: the next time you hear yourself say it, pause and ask, "Am I actually tired, or am I telling myself I'm done?" If it's real tiredness, rest. If it's the story, try five more minutes and notice what happens.

Coaching, not medical advice.

Source: Association for Psychological Science, Observer, "Need a Break? Depends on Your Concept of Willpower" (Job, Dweck & Walton, Psychological Science, 2010)
