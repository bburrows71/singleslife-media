An apology with "but" in it is an argument.

"I'm sorry, but you were yelling too."
"I'm sorry, but I was exhausted."

Whatever came before the "but," the other person hears what comes after it. And what comes after is usually the case for why it wasn't really your fault.

Your side may be real. It just belongs in a different conversation, after the apology has landed.

Try it: next time you apologize, end the sentence at the period. "I'm sorry I snapped at you." Then notice the urge to add more. What is the "but" trying to protect?
