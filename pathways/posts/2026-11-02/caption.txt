Every switch costs something.

The American Psychological Association summarizes research on task switching this way: when people switch from one task to another, they lose time. In a study by Rubinstein, Meyer and Evans, that held for every task tested, and the cost went up when the tasks were more complex or less familiar.

The researchers describe two steps behind it. First you shift the goal ("I'll do this now instead of that"). Then you switch the rules ("off for that one, on for this one"). Each switch may cost only a few tenths of a second, but going back and forth all day adds up, and brings more mistakes.

Here's the coaching part: the reach to switch is worth noticing. When does it show up for you? Right when something gets hard or uncomfortable?

Try it: pick one thing that matters today, a piece of work or a real conversation. Each time you reach for something else, pause and ask: what was I about to leave?

Source: American Psychological Association, "Multitasking: Switching costs"
