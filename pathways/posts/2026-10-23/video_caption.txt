Name it to slow it down.

In a UCLA study, 30 people looked at faces showing emotion while in a brain scanner. When they picked a word for the feeling ("angry," "fearful"), activity in the amygdala, a part of the brain tied to emotional reactions, went down. When they just picked a name for the face, it didn't.

The lead researcher said the effect is likely modest. Think brake, not off switch.

Try it: next time something hits hard, finish the sentence "Right now I feel ___." Look for the most exact word, not the closest polite one.

Coaching, not therapy.

Source: UCLA Health; Lieberman et al., "Putting Feelings Into Words," Psychological Science (2007)
